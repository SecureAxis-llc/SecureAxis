Penetration Testing

Find the gaps before someone else does.

Scoped, hands-on testing of your perimeter, internal network, web applications and cloud tenants, reported in plain language with fixes ranked by risk.

Who it's for

  • Businesses that need independent evidence of their security for a customer, insurer or audit
  • Teams launching a new application, cloud environment or remote-access setup
  • Organisations that have never had their network tested by an outsider

Problems it helps with

  • Not knowing how far an attacker could get from a single phished account
  • Customers or insurers asking for a recent penetration test report
  • Web applications and cloud tenants that have grown without a security review
  • Internal networks where one compromised laptop could reach everything

Scope and deliverables

Each test is scoped in writing before it starts: which systems, which techniques, and what is out of bounds. Testing only begins once you have signed an authorisation that lists exactly what may be tested.

What we do

  • External and internal network testing
  • Web application testing
  • Active Directory attack path review
  • Phishing and social engineering simulations

What you get

  • Executive summary and technical report
  • Findings ranked with fix steps
  • Retest to confirm fixes

How an engagement works

  1. Conversation

    You tell us what's going on and what you need. We ask questions to understand your environment and constraints.

  2. Written scope and authorisation

    We agree in writing what will be tested, how, and what is out of bounds. Testing starts only after you sign an authorisation listing exactly what may be tested.

  3. The work

    Carried out as agreed. Anything urgent is raised with you as soon as it's found, not saved for the report.

  4. Report and walkthrough

    Findings in plain language, ranked by what to fix first, and a walkthrough with your team.

Questions

Will testing disrupt our systems?

Testing is planned to avoid disruption. Risky techniques are agreed in advance or left out, and fragile systems can be tested at quieter times or excluded.

What is the difference between a penetration test and a vulnerability scan?

A scan lists known weaknesses automatically. A penetration test has a person try to use those weaknesses, chain them together and show what an attacker could actually reach.

What do we need to provide?

A contact on your side, the list of systems in scope, and a signed authorisation. For internal testing, a way to connect to your network, such as a laptop or virtual machine.
